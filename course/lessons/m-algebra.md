# Letters, expressions and balanced equations

## A letter names a quantity
If a notebook costs n dollars, three notebooks cost 3n dollars. The letter is not a unit and does not stand for the word notebook: it represents the unknown numerical price of one notebook. In one problem, keep its meaning consistent unless a change is explicitly defined.

An expression such as 3n + 2 describes a quantity. An equation such as 3n + 2 = 17 states that two quantities are equal. You can evaluate an expression when n is given; you can solve an equation to find which n makes the statement true.
## Combine like quantities
Three bags each containing x counters and two more such bags contain 3x + 2x = 5x counters. If there are also four loose counters, the expression is 5x + 4. You cannot combine that as 9x because the four loose counters are not four more bags of x.

Similarly, 7a − 2a + 3 = 5a + 3. Core simplification here involves simple linear expressions without brackets. More complicated algebra is enrichment rather than something to rush into before the meaning of a letter is secure.
## Substitution is a check of meaning
Evaluate 4n + 3 when n = 5. Four groups of five plus three give 23. The notation 4n means 4 × n, not a two-digit number with n pasted after 4. Write the multiplication sign during substitution if it helps keep the meaning visible.

For n/3 + 2 when n = 12, divide twelve by three before adding two to obtain six. Follow the same order of operations used with ordinary numbers. Algebra has not created a new arithmetic system; it has given us a way to describe relationships before every number is known.
## Solve while balancing
To solve 3x + 5 = 26, think of a balanced scale. Remove five from both sides, leaving 3x = 21. Divide both sides into three equal groups to get x = 7. Performing the same valid operation on both sides preserves their equality.

Check by substitution in the original equation: 3 × 7 + 5 = 26. Do not check only in the final simplified equation, because an error might have occurred earlier. Describing terms as 'moving to the other side' is shorthand; balancing explains why their signs or operations change.
## Translate a story
Three identical tickets and a $4 booking fee cost $31 altogether. Let t be the price of one ticket. The relationship is 3t + 4 = 31. Remove the booking fee to leave $27 for the tickets, then divide by three to find t = 9.

The answer is $9 per ticket, not simply 9 tickets. If the booking fee were $4 for each ticket instead of one fee for the order, the equation would describe a different total. Write a sentence defining every fixed and repeated amount before forming the equation.
## Diagnose and practise
A pupil solves x + 8 = 20 by subtracting eight only on the left, producing x = 20. The two sides no longer describe equal quantities after that unequal operation. Correct it by subtracting eight from both sides to get x = 12.

Now solve 5y − 6 = 29 using a bar and an equation. Restore the six first, making five equal groups total 35. Then divide by five to obtain seven. Show that the pictorial and symbolic methods preserve the same relationship.
## Explain and transfer
Invent a real situation described by 4p + 6 = 38. Specify what p measures, what is repeated four times and what the six represents. Solve it, then check that the solution makes practical sense in your invented setting.

Before independent practice, explain the difference between an expression and an equation, and justify one balancing step in words. Algebra becomes a useful reasoning language when you can connect symbols to quantities and verify their meaning.
