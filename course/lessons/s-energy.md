# Energy forms and conversions

## Energy helps explain change
Energy is needed for processes such as movement, heating, producing light and living activities. We describe useful forms including kinetic, potential, electrical, light, heat and sound energy at primary level. The choice of form depends on the situation being explained.

Kinetic energy is associated with movement. Potential energy is associated with position or stored conditions, such as a raised object or a stretched spring. Food and fuels store chemical potential energy. Energy is not the same as a material that can be weighed in a lunch box.
## A conversion chain
In a battery-powered torch, chemical potential energy in the battery is converted to electrical energy, then to light and heat. The battery does not contain a miniature beam of light waiting to travel down the wire. Naming conversions explains how different parts contribute.

A device often produces more than one output. A motor may produce movement as well as sound and heat. “Useful output” depends on the intended purpose; heat is useful in a heater but often an unwanted output in a motor.
## Movement and stored energy
Raise a book and its gravitational potential energy increases relative to the chosen lower position. If it falls, this can be converted into kinetic energy. When it stops on a surface, energy is transferred to the surroundings, including as heat and sound.

The energy does not simply vanish because motion stops. However, you need not calculate kinetic or potential energy using secondary-school formulas in this primary unit. Focus on identifying the relevant changes and the system involved.
## Living things and sunlight
Green plants convert light energy into chemical potential energy stored in food through photosynthesis. Animals obtain food by feeding on plants or other organisms. Respiration releases energy from food for life processes.

A food chain therefore also represents energy transfer. Matter and energy must not be treated as identical: organisms take in materials, use them and release wastes, while energy is transferred and becomes less available for subsequent useful processes. The detailed energy budget is extension work.
## Worked example: a wind-up toy
Winding a toy stores elastic potential energy in its spring. When released, the spring drives moving parts, producing kinetic energy. Some energy is also transferred as sound and heat. A toy stopping does not prove that energy has been destroyed.

Compare it with a battery toy. Both can produce movement, but the initial stored energy and conversion pathway differ. A good answer identifies the actual source instead of writing the same generic chain for every moving object.
## Evidence and boundaries
When describing a conversion, define the system. If you study only the bulb, electrical energy enters and light and heat leave. If you include the battery, begin with the battery’s stored chemical energy. Both descriptions can be useful when their boundaries are clear.

Do not add energy outputs solely because they are on a memorised list. Name those relevant to the device and observations. Claims about exact efficiency require measurements, so an illustration of a bright bulb cannot establish a numerical efficiency.
## Explain and transfer
Choose a familiar appliance and trace input, conversion and outputs. Identify its useful output and one additional output. Explain why a warm casing is evidence of heat transfer, not proof that the device has stopped obeying energy principles.

For an unfamiliar machine, follow what supplies it, what moves or changes, and what reaches the surroundings. This systematic approach is more reliable than guessing a chain from the machine’s name.
