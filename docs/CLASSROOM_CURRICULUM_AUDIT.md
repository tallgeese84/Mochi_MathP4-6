Its FAQ describes application of school knowledge, wider reading, problem-solving, independence, creativity and communication. A detailed public entrance syllabus with topic weights, thresholds or an official question blueprint was not found in these sources. **The MOE topic mapping below is our preparation framework, not an official NUS High exam syllabus.**

The admissions page reviewed described the 2026 exercise, including July tests and camp. Those dates are not dates for a future 2027 exercise. For a planned secondary-school start after September 2027 SPERS, monitor the earlier DSA application window separately. Check the school’s rules for applicants outside Singapore mainstream schools, report-book equivalents and the relevant MOE registration route; this course cannot determine eligibility from school location alone.

## What was missing in the app

The v4.4 question studio already had generated questions, worked solutions, targeted repair and Science models. A worked solution appeared after a problem; it was not a sequenced teaching course. Science’s eight activity areas omitted substantial primary content such as classification, materials, life cycles, reproduction and magnets. Its 36 initial items were not comprehensive syllabus coverage. The Maths difficulty band could jump after repeated independent answers in the same format without retaining the actual difficulty of each attempt.

v5 adds the following original teaching units before its classroom checks. Each Markdown source is under `course/lessons/`; the exact source page references and prerequisite links are in `course/catalog.json`.

The primary treatment does not require detailed heart chambers/valves, alveoli, naming xylem/phloem/stomata, pollen-tube mechanisms, umbilical-cord anatomy or numerical acceleration formulas. These exclusions prevent advanced terminology from becoming a false gate to primary understanding. Accurate synonyms are accepted. A concept being useful enrichment does not make it an official selection-test requirement.

## Wording and illustration decisions

Ice gains heat and melts into liquid water; it remains the substance water. Water vapour is invisible; mist contains droplets. Temperature and heat are distinguished, including constant temperature during melting under specified conditions. Evaporation and boiling are different. Plants make food using light energy, water and carbon dioxide; roots absorb water and mineral salts. Respiration differs from breathing. Current is not consumed by the first bulb. Food-chain arrows represent energy transfer from food to consumer.

Question conditions were tightened during review: equal sharing is explicit; identical bottles are not assumed to hold equal volumes; bead packs use all beads without leftovers; water-depth calculations specify a cuboid and, where relevant, an initially empty tank. Explanation text connects operations to their meaning instead of presenting a bare answer.

Original raster art provides observational context. It is not evidence of species identification, exact anatomy, forces, dimensions or measured outcomes. Precise diagrams are separately generated from checked relationships. A generated five-square pseudo-net was rejected; the retained artwork uses a plain notebook, and the exact six-square net was independently checked by folding its faces mathematically. Flow labels and arrows have their own layout space. The plant experiment illustration is explicitly a flawed proposed setup to critique, not a claimed experimental result.

## Verification and remaining scope

The checks validate unit prerequisites, question structure and stable answer reordering; they recompute selected mathematical models, verify the cube net independently, and exercise promotion, recovery, review, drafts, notebook export and device merges. The full app startup test covers a stalled cloud connection, classroom-before-practice, wrong-answer revision, subject switching and existing question-studio behaviour.

This coverage does not certify every teaching sentence through an independent educator, calibrate difficulty to NUS High applicants, or assess a child’s spoken explanations and practical work. There are six introductory checks per unit, not a large unseen assessment bank for every outcome. Use teacher review and unfamiliar external tasks to decide whether additional examples or explanations are needed. There is no basis for claiming a percentile or predicting admission from app results.
