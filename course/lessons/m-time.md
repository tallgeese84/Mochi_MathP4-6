# Time is a number line with a different base

## Distinguish a time from a duration
At 14:35 describes a moment on a clock. For 35 minutes describes a duration. Subtracting two moments can produce a duration, and adding a duration to a moment can produce a new moment. These quantities play different roles even when their written numbers look similar.

An hour contains sixty minutes, and a minute contains sixty seconds. Clock arithmetic is not ordinary decimal arithmetic. The notation 1 hour 30 minutes means one and a half hours, not 1.30 hours. Convert units or use a timeline to keep the relationship clear.
## Read the clock unambiguously
In the 24-hour system, 09:15 is morning and 21:15 is evening. Noon is 12:00 and midnight begins a new day at 00:00. In 12-hour notation, 12 noon is 12 p.m. and 12 midnight is 12 a.m.; when confusion is possible, write noon or midnight explicitly.

To convert 3:40 p.m. to 24-hour time, add twelve to the hour to get 15:40. Do not add twelve to 12 noon. For 00:25, write 12:25 a.m. in the 12-hour system. The date becomes relevant whenever an interval crosses midnight.
## Bridge to a convenient boundary
A lesson begins at 10:45 and ends at 12:20. From 10:45 to 11:00 is 15 minutes; from 11:00 to 12:00 is 60 minutes; from 12:00 to 12:20 is 20 minutes. Total duration is 95 minutes, or one hour 35 minutes.

The timeline makes regrouping visible. A written subtraction method must borrow one hour as sixty minutes, not as one hundred. Both methods work if they respect the unit relationship. Choose a method that lets you explain every step rather than one that merely resembles whole-number subtraction.
## Work backwards
A train arrives at 16:10 after a journey of one hour 45 minutes. Subtract an hour to reach 15:10, then subtract ten minutes to 15:00 and thirty-five more to 14:25. The departure time was 14:25.

Check forwards: 14:25 plus one hour is 15:25, plus 45 minutes is 16:10. In reverse-time problems, this forward check is especially useful because it detects mistakes around hour boundaries. If there is a separate waiting period, label it as a separate segment.
## Cross midnight carefully
A trip begins at 23:35 and ends at 00:20 the next day. There are 25 minutes until midnight and 20 minutes afterwards, giving 45 minutes. Simply subtracting the displayed hour values would give a meaningless negative duration.

For multi-day contexts, include the date or an explicit 'next day' label. Do not invent a next-day assumption if the problem is ambiguous. Good mathematical communication includes clarifying the timeline before calculating rather than hiding uncertainty inside an answer.
## Guided planning task
You need to arrive at an appointment at 15:00. Travel takes 35 minutes and getting ready takes 20 minutes. Work backwards to depart at 14:25 and begin getting ready at 14:05. Draw three labelled moments and the two durations between them.

Now add a desired ten-minute arrival buffer. The appointment still begins at 15:00, but your target arrival is 14:50, departure 14:15 and preparation start 13:55. Explain why the buffer changes your plan without changing the actual travel duration.
## Explain and transfer
Make a short timetable containing two activities, a break and a journey. Ask someone to find a missing start time or duration. Check their answer by tracing the entire sequence forwards and ensuring the total duration equals the sum of all segments.

Before independent practice, name the start, end, duration and date when relevant. Decide whether the unknown is a clock reading or an amount of time, then give it in the appropriate notation. That small distinction prevents many otherwise avoidable errors.
