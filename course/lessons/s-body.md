# Digestion: from food to absorbed nutrients

## Why digest food?
Food supplies materials for growth and repair and is a source from which the body releases energy. Much of the food we eat must be broken down into simpler substances that can be absorbed. Digestion is this breakdown; absorption is the movement of suitable digested substances into the blood.

Chewing food into smaller pieces helps but is not the whole digestive process. A food item becoming smaller does not automatically mean all its useful substances have entered the bloodstream. Keep breakdown, absorption and transport as connected but distinct ideas.
## Mouth and gullet
Teeth break food into smaller pieces in the mouth. Saliva moistens food and begins digestion of some components. The tongue helps move food for swallowing. Different parts contribute different actions, so the mouth is more than an opening into the body.

The gullet carries swallowed food to the stomach. It is not the windpipe. Mixing these names can produce an incorrect explanation of both digestion and breathing. Trace food and air along separate routes in your own labelled diagrams.
## Stomach
The stomach mixes food and continues digestion. Its muscular action and digestive juices help process the food. It does not simply store an unchanged meal until it passes out of the body.

The stomach is also not where a baby develops; that is the womb. Scientific vocabulary prevents everyday phrases such as “baby in the stomach” from confusing different body systems. At primary level, focus on the sequence and main functions rather than memorising names of digestive chemicals.
## Small intestine
Digestion continues in the small intestine, and digested food substances are absorbed into the blood there. Blood then transports useful substances to the rest of the body. This connects the digestive and circulatory systems.

The small intestine is not called “small” because it is shorter than the large intestine. Its name refers to its narrower width. You do not need detailed microscopic structures to explain the main primary function: digestion and absorption of digested food.
## Large intestine and undigested material
The large intestine absorbs water from the remaining contents. Undigested material eventually leaves the body as faeces through the anus. Do not describe all faeces as unused digested food: some material was not digested and absorbed.

Removing undigested material is different from releasing energy from food in cells. Food does not disappear when eaten; materials move through processes and are used, transported or removed. Careful tracking avoids the idea that the stomach turns everything directly into energy.
## Worked journey of a meal
Trace a piece of bread: it is chewed and mixed with saliva in the mouth, travels along the gullet, undergoes further processing in the stomach, and reaches the small intestine where digestion and absorption occur. Remaining material passes to the large intestine, where water is absorbed.

Now ask what happens after absorption. Useful substances in the blood reach body parts that need them. An answer ending at the stomach misses the key connection between eating and supplying the rest of the body. Different food components are processed differently, so this is a simplified system account.
## Model and explain
Draw the organs in sequence with arrows that mean movement of food contents. Separately draw an arrow from the small intestine to blood, labelled absorption of digested food. This second arrow represents a different movement, not the next segment of the tube.

Explain why a digestive problem affecting absorption can matter even if someone eats enough food. The body must obtain usable substances, not merely take food into the mouth. This reasoning links organ function to whole-body consequences without requiring a memorised medical diagnosis.
